🎬 Lessons 🗓 Lesson planner ✋ Participation 🧭 Teacher guide

About half the class answers in a lesson. Most of the rest are not refusing: the step from watching to doing is too big, or the words are. These tools make that step smaller. They are on every lesson, for every student, with nothing to switch on.

What students now have

Dotted words: tap for the meaning

Math and instruction words on every slide have a dotted line under them (slope, y-intercept, at most, divide, solve and about fifty more). A tap shows one plain sentence, an example or a small picture, and the word in the student's language: Arabic, Korean, Chinese, Spanish or French. The student picks the language once. You can tap a word on the class screen too.

The translations are standard school math terms, but have a speaker of each language look over the word wall below before you rely on them.

🪜 Help me start: three steps on every question

  1. Understand the question. The key words with meanings, the question cut into short pieces, and one first move.
  2. See one like it. The worked example the class just did, with every blank filled in. In older lessons, the teaching slide for that idea.
  3. Set it up. The set-up for a number question, one wrong option crossed out for multiple choice, or a sentence starter that drops into the answer box for a written question.

Help never changes a score, and the student is told so. Each step is asked for separately, so a student takes only as much as they need. On the exit ticket the worked example is left out: it is your evidence.

🔊 Read to me, A Text size, 📖 Words

Three buttons at the top of the student's page. Read to me reads the slide aloud in the browser's own voice and highlights each part as it goes (headphones help). Text size has three steps. Words lists every dotted word in the lesson with its meaning: use it as a pre-teaching list.

What changed in the Unit 2 review lessons

  • Three retrieval questions at the start, one minute each, on the small skills that day's work needs. If under half the class gets one right, fix it then, before the new work.
  • I do → we do → you do. In each skill the last example is now a we do: the first move is done and the class finishes it. The console marks these slides.
  • A check after each skill with a move-on rule (in the planner): 80% right, move on; 50 to 79%, one more together; under 50%, reteach.
  • Optional slides are marked on the console and in the planner, so you know what to skip when time is short.

A routine for the students who are not answering

  1. Before the lesson (2 minutes). Open the planner, load the last lesson's results and read "Not started" and "Started, then stopped". Pick three names. Write them in the plan's note box.
  2. Retrieval warm-up. Stand next to those three. The goal is one typed answer each before you walk away. A wrong answer counts: it is a start.
  3. I do. Lock the slides. Everyone types the blanks before you reveal a step. Say "tap the dotted word" each time a key word appears.
  4. We do. Pairs, with one of your three next to a student who got the last check right. Same-language pairs may talk in that language. Cold call a pair, not a person.
  5. Check. Two silent minutes. Use the console's "has not answered" list: go to each name and say only "Press Help me start, step one". Do not explain yet. Come back in a minute.
  6. After the check. Use the move-on rule. Students who were wrong twice join you during practice with the we-do example again; the others go on to the ★ extras.
  7. Exit ticket. For your three: sentence starter first (Help me start, step 3). One sentence is success today.
  8. End. A team game (Lane Clash) only after the exit tickets are in. The student's last slide shows how many times they used the ladder: praise it.
