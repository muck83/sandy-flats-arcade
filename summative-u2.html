Unit 2 Summative · Linear equations and inequalities

The Pop-Up Arcade

Name   Class

This summer, the Sandy Flats Arcade is opening a pop-up arcade in the old boathouse on the beach. Priya, Marcus and Gus have to decide what goes inside. They can rent two kinds of machines for the summer: claw machines and air hockey tables. These are the limits they have to work with.

  • Floor space. A claw machine takes up 2 m² of floor. An air hockey table takes up 6 m², including room for the players. The boathouse has 60 m² of floor for machines.
  • Budget. Renting a claw machine for the summer costs $200. Renting an air hockey table costs $400. The arcade has given them $4800 to spend on machines.
  • Prize deal. The prize company only delivers prizes if the pop-up has at least 4 claw machines.
  • Stock. There are only 8 air hockey tables for rent in town.
  • Staff pay. The pop-up must take in more than $300 a day to pay its two summer staff. On a normal day, one claw machine takes in $20 and one air hockey table takes in $50.
The questionHow many claw machines and how many air hockey tables should they rent, so that the pop-up takes in the most money each day?

The three of them already disagree:

Marcus"Easy. Ten of each. Fair to both machines."
Priya"Claw machines are small and cheap. Rent as many claw machines as we can, and nothing else."
Gus"Air hockey takes in more. Rent all 8 air hockey tables, then fill the space that's left with claw machines."

By the end of this task you will know who is right, or whether there is a better plan than all three.

Use these letters the whole way through: c = the number of claw machines, h = the number of air hockey tables. You can only rent whole machines.

Choose your format

📄 The planOne or two pages, on paper or typed: the classic report to the arcade.
🪧 The posterOne A3 poster that could hang in the boathouse office, with the graph as the centrepiece.
🎬 The pitchA 90-second video pitch to the arcade. Show your graph and table on screen while you talk, and upload a photo of them too.

Your plan must include (in every format)

  1. The question in your own words, and what c and h stand for.
  2. The limits table: each limit in words, its inequality, and (for the prize deal and the stock limit) its number line.
  3. The system of inequalities.
  4. The graph: labelled axes, every boundary line labelled, solid and dashed lines used correctly, the feasible region shaded, its corners marked, and the three friends' plans marked.
  5. The corner table with the money each corner takes in.
  6. Your recommendation: a short paragraph to the arcade that names the plan, gives the money it takes in, says why it is better than Marcus's, Priya's and Gus's plans, and answers the what-if question and your news flash.
How to hand it in
  • On paper: take a clear photo of every page (flat, in good light, all of the page in the picture), or scan it to one PDF.
  • On this page: press Print or save my work as PDF at the top and choose Save as PDF. Check your graph photo is in it.
  • A pitch video: upload the video, plus a photo of your graph and table.
  • Upload the photo or PDF to the Unit 2 Summative assignment on Toddle. Check that it opens and that every page is readable.
Final check before you upload:
  • All six parts of the plan are there.
  • The graph has labels, the dashed line is dashed, and the region is shaded.
  • Your recommendation uses numbers from your table.
  • Your name is on every page.
