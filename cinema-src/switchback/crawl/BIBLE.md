# Under the Pond: series bible (draft 5, for review)

This replaces the cable-car story in `../BIBLE.md`. There is no mountain and no cable car. Nothing has been built
from it. *Under the Pond* is a working title.

## Premise

Marcus has fallen in the Sandy Flats duck pond four times. The fourth time, a calm voice says "Application
accepted", the pond drains, and he goes down with it. Doug picks up Marcus's helmet and drops in after him. Under
the pond are nine floors, each with a rule, and the only way out is down. Every wrong answer that put Marcus in the
pond was counted as an application. Two students went down eleven years ago. Neither came back, and one of them has
been rebuilding the floors ever since.

Film 0 is how they get in. After that each floor is one step of the Units 2 to 4 plan. A floor with a lot of maths
is told in two short parts.

**In one sentence:** a show-off student and a pigeon who takes safety very seriously fall through the duck pond into
nine floors of tests under their town, and the only way out is down.

## What to protect

1. **The pitch fits in one sentence.** Each film restates it in under ten seconds.
2. **Two leads who need each other.** Marcus is brave, quick and careless. Doug is exact, slow and stops dead when
   there is no procedure. Each film needs one moment where Marcus is right and Doug isn't, and one the other way.
   Doug may leave at any time. He never does.
3. **Rules the class can think along with.** Each floor's rule is stated before anyone acts. Things that look minor
   come back later, and every floor uses a tool from an earlier one.
4. **The joke and the danger have one source.** The floors are an assessment, and the Voice treats every mistake as
   paperwork. The same wrong answer is a neat incident slip to the Voice and one step closer to staying for Marcus.
5. **Voices nobody could confuse.** Marcus loud and fast, the Voice level and polite, Doug dry and exact. Audition
   them by ear before building.
6. **The class is inside the story.** The Voice says the observers are being assessed too. Each lesson the class
   votes on one standing question: who is the Foreman? The count opens the next lesson.

## How a floor works

- **The floor has a rule, and the rule is the maths.**
- **The Voice** states each task and files an incident slip for each mistake: "Incident. Cause: start minus end.
  Penalty: goats. Incidents on this floor: one of three."
- **Three incidents on a floor and you stay on it.** Do not let Marcus reach two of three on every floor: vary it,
  let Doug take one, and on one floor let them hit three and lose something real.
- **The penalty is goats.** Keith works in the pen.
- **Tips go on the helmet,** one line per floor.
- **The Foreman acts on every floor, on screen:** he moves track, takes maps, rebuilds what is behind them.

## The answer (for writers; never stated before Floor 9)

Eleven years ago two students from Mr. Dunmore's class fell through the pond together.

- **The one in the cart** had three incidents on Floor One. The third was at the last door: the right numbers,
  subtracted in different orders top and bottom. An entrant who stays on a floor becomes part of it. That student is
  now **the Voice**.
- **The other one** passed Floor One and kept going. He passed eight floors and stopped at the ninth, because
  passing it means leaving, and his friend cannot leave. He is **the Foreman**. He rebuilds every floor and makes it
  harder so that nobody passes the ninth and closes the place with the Voice still inside it.
- **Mr. Dunmore** was at the pond that day and did not go in after them. Doug did go in after Marcus. That is what
  Dunmore has never said, and it is why the season ends with him at the edge of the hole.

The Foreman is wrong about what happens at the bottom, and the last floor's maths (a region where every limit holds
at once) is how Marcus and Doug show him a way out that includes both of them.

Suspects for the class vote, all kept plausible until Floor 7: the student in the cart, the other student, Mr.
Dunmore, the Voice, Keith.

## Words

- Marcus and Doug are **entrants**. The place is "under the pond" or "the floors". Mistakes are **incidents**.
- **Level** means the lift only. On a track, graphs and tables read **posts** and **height**.
- "Minus" is the sign; "take away" is the operation.
- Say "change in height over change in posts" at least once in any film that finds a slope, and show ordered
  pairs on screen as (post, height).

## Floor One's numbers

- **Lift:** from level 4 to −2 (change −6); from −2 to 3 (change +5).
- **Track:** (1, 2) to (4, 8), slope 2; (4, 8) to (8, 8), slope 0; (8, 8) to (10, 4), slope −2.
  Span one is kept as (1, 2) to (4, 8) on purpose: it is the Film 0 quiz question and the pair from Rewind. Both
  points lie on y = 2x, so "end height over end post" also gives 2 there. That mistake has to be caught another way
  (the game checks the boxes; the lesson has a worked step) and is exposed on the later spans.
- **Exercise book:** (10, 4), (11, 6), (14, 12), (15, 14). Post gaps +1, +3, +1; height gaps +2, +6, +2; slope 2.
- **Stairs:** (2, −1) to (6, −3), slope −1/2.
